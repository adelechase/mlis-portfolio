Research

Students will be able to perform effectively in the library and information science professions by demonstrating competency in research activities. By the conclusion of the master's degree program, students should be able to:

  1. identify and explain a research question

  2. locate, evaluate, analyze, and synthesize research findings

  3. identify appropriate research methods, collect, and analyze data to address issues faced by libraries or other information agencies

  4. employ evidence-based practices to solve information problems

  5. design, conduct, and report research that contributes to the body of professional knowledge and/or theoretical constructs

Description

For this assignment, I created a research proposal for a study entitled, Autistic Postsecondary Students’ Perceptions of Sensory Spaces in Academic Libraries. The purpose of the proposal was to design an empirical research project proposal from beginning to end, including the research problem, literature review, theoretical framework, methodology, ethics, data collection plan, data analysis plan, and appendices. My proposed study focused on autistic college and university students and how they experience sensory spaces in academic libraries. I was interested in this topic because academic libraries often present themselves as welcoming, inclusive spaces, but there is still limited research on whether sensory spaces actually meet the needs of the autistic students they are meant to support.

Analysis

In my proposal, I focused on the need to better understand how autistic postsecondary students experience sensory spaces, identifying that as a research question with specific relevance in academic librarianship with implications for accessibility, inclusion, and student support. The proposal shows my ability to find and work with existing research, since I used scholarship on autism, sensory environments, academic library accessibility, self-efficacy, and disability studies to establish what is already known, where the gaps remain, and why my proposed study would be useful. I synthesized these disparate topics into a larger argument about the need to center autistic students’ own perspectives. Finally, the proposal demonstrates my ability to choose research methods that fit a real problem faced by libraries. By designing a mixed-methods study that includes surveys, Photovoice, focus groups, demographic data, and both quantitative and qualitative analysis, I showed how research methods can be used to collect meaningful data and translate that data into practical insight for improving library spaces and services.

Reflection

This assignment was very difficult because it required me to think through an entire research project on my own, and not just the topic or the argument. I had to move my mindset from “this is an issue I care about” to “this is a researchable problem with questions, methods, instruments, ethics, analysis, limitations, and implications.” That was a much bigger shift than I expected. I also found it challenging to balance the quantitative and qualitative parts of the study. I wanted the project to include measurable data about self-efficacy, but I also wanted to make sure that autistic students’ own voices and experiences stayed at the center of the research. Photovoice felt like the right method for that, but it also made the ethical and practical parts of the proposal more complicated. In retrospect, I definitely bit off almost more than I could chew with this proposal, and I probably should have narrowed the scope of the project a little more. The proposal became ambitious very quickly, especially with mixed methods. In a real-world version of this project, I would likely start with a smaller qualitative study first, then build toward a larger mixed methods project later. Overall, though, this assignment helped me understand how much planning goes into ethical, useful research. It pushed me to connect my professional values around accessibility and inclusion with an actual research design.
