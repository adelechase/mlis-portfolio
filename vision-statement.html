Vision Statement

Over the course of my MLIS degree, my vision for librarianship has become both more focused and more expansive. When I began this program, I imagined my future primarily in academic libraries. That goal still matters to me. I remain drawn to the teaching, research support, information literacy work, and intellectual community that academic libraries make possible. However, my current work as a Research and Readers’ Advisory Professional in a public library has shown me that the kind of librarianship I value is not limited to one type of institution. I am fulfilled by work that allows me to teach, connect people with information, help patrons feel more confident navigating the world around them, and fulfill their desires for entertainment and emotional release.

​

In my current role, I have the opportunity to practice many of the skills that first drew me toward academic librarianship. I teach technology classes, provide one-on-one research assistance, create instructional materials, and think carefully about how people learn. Instructional design has become a central part of how I understand librarianship: making information usable, accessible, and meaningful. Whether I am helping someone build a website, understand Microsoft 365, locate reliable sources, or breaking down an intimidating technology task into manageable steps, I am developing highly transferable skills in teaching, research support, curriculum design, and user-centered service. 

 

At the same time, public librarianship reminds me every day that information needs are deeply human. Patrons come to the library with questions that may seem small on the surface but matter enormously in their lives. They need help applying for jobs, using devices, finding books that speak to them, researching personal questions, understanding systems, or solving problems they may not even know how to name yet. Readers’ advisory is part of that same work. Recommending a book is not only about matching genre or plot; as with research questions, it is about listening closely, honoring the patron’s tastes and experiences, and helping them find the emotions they seek: pleasure, recognition, escape, challenge, or comfort.

​

My vision of librarianship is grounded in equity, curiosity, and conscientious service. I want to be the kind of librarian who pays attention: to barriers, to language, to access, to whose stories are visible, and to who feels welcome asking for help. I believe libraries are strongest when they treat patrons not as problems to be solved, but as people with knowledge, agency, and complex lives. The diverse patrons I work with every day continue to shape my understanding of what inclusive service actually requires: patience, humility, creativity, and a willingness to meet people where they are.

 

I see my professional future as one built around connection between people, information, and possibility. Whether I remain in public libraries, move into academic librarianship, or find a role that blends both, I want my work to reflect the same commitments: thoughtful instruction, ethical research support, intellectual freedom, diverse collections, and practical problem-solving. My MLIS has given me the framework to understand this work more deeply, but my day-to-day service has taught me why it matters. My vision is to continue growing as a librarian who helps people feel capable, informed, seen, and empowered.
